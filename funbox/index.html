What the offers can be ↓

The part I am most excited about

You don't do discounts. Nothing here asks you to.

Your own FAQ says it plainly: FunBox does not offer coupons or discounts. That is a good policy and I would not touch it. It also happens to be why an attraction is a better fit for this than a restaurant will ever be.

A restaurant rewarding a guest has to give away food, which is real margin every time. You can hand a family something they genuinely want that costs you a fraction of a ticket, and never once cut your price.

FUNBOX grip socks on usSocks are required and families forget them constantly. Turning a small annoyance at the desk into a gift is the cheapest goodwill in the building.
Free arcade tokensReads as a present to a kid. Costs a fraction of a ticket, and it pulls the family toward spending more once they are already inside.
Front of the line at check-inCosts nothing at all. Feels like VIP treatment to a nine year old, who is the person actually deciding whether you come back.
A weekday session on the houseAimed only at the sessions that do not sell out. You are filling a seat that was going to sit empty, and the family almost never arrives alone or leaves without buying something.
A free slushie or snackLow cost, high perceived value, and it lands exactly where families are already spending.
A birthday party add-onThrow an extra into a booked party instead of shaving the price. You protect the rate and still look generous.

The rule I use: pick the reward with the widest gap between what it is worth to the family and what it costs you.

A percent-off coupon fails that test, because it costs you exactly what it is worth, and it is against your policy anyway. Socks, tokens, a skip-the-line or an off-peak seat all pass it easily. Nothing is locked in: we run one offer this month, a different one next month, and keep whatever fills the park.
